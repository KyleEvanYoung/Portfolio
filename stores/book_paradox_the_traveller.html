Back Story Of The Book

I wanted to write a novel capable of exploring grand ideas—knights and their kingdoms, aliens on distant worlds, and the far future—all woven together by a time‑travel plot. At its core is a man who’s willing to sacrifice everything to achieve his goals, all while hiding a secret from his past. This journey launches us into the universe of Paradox, a multiverse governed by five magics—Soul, Time, Reality, Life, and Death—and builds inexorably toward an epic confrontation with a mysterious adversary named Parallax.

About the Book

In a fusion of destinies and mystical quests, Noah, known as ‘The Traveler,’ embarks on a perilous journey through time in search of magical elements essential for mastering the forces of creation. Alongside him, Joshua, an assassin from the future, grapples with his broken past and the haunting memories that threaten to overshadow his lethal skills. Meanwhile, Edward confronts the loss of his magical prowess and navigates his new existence as a dragon, a form both powerful and alien to him. These three extraordinary characters, each dealing with their own set of challenges, find their fates intertwined as they navigate the complexities of loyalty, power, and identity in a multiverse orchestrated by unseen forces. Together, they face an uncertain future, their paths marked by trials of magic, betrayal, and redemption.

Prologue: The Prophecy of Parallax

In one of the ancient tales of the Seer, it was whispered that the universe did not erupt from

a mere burst of energy. Still, it was instead woven into existence by five celestial beings,

each an embodiment of a distinct type of magic—the fundamental elements of creation.

Soul magic (green): This magic dwells within every creature, forming the essence of their

being. Known as green magic, it binds the soul to a spirit animal. Those gifted in soul magic

can harness their bonded creature’s powers to augment their abilities or in the hands of the

truly powerful, summon these creatures to aid in battle or provide companionship.

Time magic (blue): Blue magic flows through the continuum, shaping our journey from one

moment to the next. While glimpsing the past is within reach for many, altering it—travelling

back in time, pausing, or hastening events—remains a formidable challenge. Central to time

magic is the mastery of one’s time stream, a hidden current that, if understood, allows one to

manipulate the length and quality of their existence.

Reality magic (red): Red magic moulds the physical world. It grants its wielders the power to

transform matter, changing one substance into another with a mere thought. Though altering

existing forms is feasible; creating something entirely new demands immense strength and

skill, as does porting across the spans of space.

Life magic (white): The essence of existence, white magic, governs the forces of life. Simple

healing comes naturally to its practitioners, but reviving the dead or invoking new life from

the void approaches the boundaries of impossibility without mastery over both life and death

itself.

Death magic (black): Also known as black magic, it emerges naturally at the cusp of death.

This enigmatic and perilous magic thrives on the life force of others, allowing its masters to

commune with the dead or extend their own lives at the cost of another’s vitality. In the

hands of a supreme adept, it holds the terrifying power to extinguish thousands of lives in an

instant.

Together, these beings crafted a multiverse, each nurturing their own universes. Yet over

eons, they grew weary as they watched civilisations repeat the same mistakes. In their

desire for change, they created Parallax—a being capable of controlling any entity across

the multiverse to prevent self-destruction. However, their creation backfired when Parallax

chose domination over guidance, stripping beings of their free will.

In desperation, four of the magical entities—Life, Reality, Soul, and Time—forged an alliance

against Parallax. Recognising his immense power, they gathered an army from across time

and space, sparking the Dark War. Victory was hard-fought, and though they could not

destroy Parallax, they managed to imprison him in a hidden reality, sealing away their

powers into objects to guard them against misuse.

Legend holds that Parallax will return, heralded by two dark beings, and only a group of

heroes—the guardian, the king, the traveller, the survivor, the watcher, and the thief—can

hope to oppose him. But the prophecy foretells that their success will come at a great cost.

Chapter 1: Echoes of a Kingdom

Genesis, Merlin’s School, Year 3, 22 November, 10 pm

Hope fled the crowded hall, the sounds of laughter and celebration fading as she escaped

into the cool night. The wooden bench near the edge of the garden called to her, a refuge

from the chaos of her eighteenth birthday party—a party she no longer felt was hers. This

year, like the ones before, she shared the day with everyone except Noah, Charlie, Edward,

and Evan. But the weight of those absent and what was lost made her heart ache.

She slumped onto the bench, clutching a delicate necklace with trembling hands. The locket

swung open to reveal a tiny picture of her family, a frozen moment from a life that now felt

impossibly far away. Tears blurred her vision, spilling onto her cheeks as she stared at the

image. It had been years since she’d seen them, yet the ache hadn’t dulled.

Noah followed her outside, his sharp eyes catching her small figure hunched against the

bench. The sight of her crying hit him like a blow, and he approached softly, his steps

crunching on the gravel.

‘Hope?’ he called gently, his voice barely louder than the whisper of the wind.

She didn’t look up but held the necklace tighter as if drawing strength from it. Noah sat

beside her, his presence a silent offering of comfort. He didn’t ask what was wrong; he knew.

Instead, he stood and extended his hand towards her with a lopsided grin.

‘Dance with me,’ he said, his voice light though his heart felt heavy for her.

She looked at him, confusion knitting her brows. ‘Noah, I don’t—’

‘Please,’ he interrupted softly, a flicker of their shared saying lighting his gaze.

‘As the birds sing . . .’—Hope hesitated, her lips trembling, before completing it in a broken

whisper—‘the skies open to show us the way home.’

She allowed him to pull her to her feet. Slowly, he twirled her under the open sky, the nebula

above casting an ethereal glow over them. He hummed softly, guiding her into a rhythm only

they could hear. The weight of her sorrow began to lift, just slightly, as they swayed in the

night.

For a moment, she let herself smile. ‘You’re ridiculous,’ she murmured, resting her head

against his shoulder.

‘I’ve been told,’ Noah said, his voice warm. ‘But I’d rather be ridiculous than see you sad.’

The moment felt perfect until Noah’s gaze flickered to the edge of the garden. There beyond

the comforting light of the nebula, a shadow loomed. It was human-like, but not a formless

figure that seemed to shift with the wind. It stood motionless, watching.

Noah froze, his breath hitching. His heart raced, a memory rising unbidden of the day his

father had passed away when he’d first seen it, the monster. It hadn’t come closer then, but

it hadn’t gone either. The sight of it now twisted his gut, an unease clawing at him.

‘Noah?’ Hope’s voice pulled him back, her hands gripping his. ‘What’s wrong?’

He forced a smile, shaking his head. ‘Nothing,’ he lied. ‘Let’s go back inside.’

Multiverse of Time, Earth, Somerset, Kingdom of Camelot, Year 1232, 24 July, 2am

The sky erupted in a vivid blaze of green as Camelot’s magical shield strained under the fury

of the dragon’s relentless assault. The ground trembled with the beast’s wrath, shaking the

castle to its core. In the dim confines of Guinevere’s chamber, tension clung to the air like a

pacing, the old wooden floorboards groaned louder with each step, echoing his growing

despair.

‘Galahad, cease your pacing!’ Lohengrin's voice broke the strained silence, taut with

concentration.

Galahad stopped, his eyes piercing through the dim light as they fixed on Lohengrin. ‘How

long does she have?’ he demanded, urgency seeping into every word.

Lohengrin, his face lined with exhaustion and the shadow of grief dulling his eyes, shook his

faintly glowing with the remnants of white magic trembled. ‘But . . . not long.’

‘Can't you give her more time?’ Galahad’s voice cracked, desperation creeping through his

voice as his hands clenched into fists.

Lohengrin’s gaze drifted to the cold stone walls, his lips curling into a wistful smile as a tear

slid down his cheek. ‘I could, but we both know that’s not what she wants.’

Galahad fell silent, his thoughts swirling like a storm until a sudden cough from the bed jolted

him from his turmoil. Guinevere’s frail hand reached out, clutching Lohengrin’s trembling

fingers. Her voice, barely audible, was laced with affection. ‘My sons, though we gather

under such grim circumstances, I’m glad to call you family.’

Galahad wiped his eyes, his voice low as he whispered, ‘Thank you for taking us in.’

Lohengrin, his tone filled with gratitude, added, ‘After my parents’ deaths, you taught me

what family truly means.’

Galahad stepped towards the foot of the bed, his voice softer now. ‘Thank you for everything

you’ve done.’

Guinevere’s faint smile brightened the room, warming their hearts. ‘My boys,’ she said, her

voice tender, ‘come here.’ She lifted her arms, weak but welcoming, and the two men

embraced her gently. Lohengrin blinked back his tears as he held her close. After a long

moment, they released her, and Galahad vowed, ‘We will protect the kingdom.’

Guinevere nodded, her eyes soft with trust. ‘I know you will.’ Then with concern, she asked,

‘Where is Loholt?’

Galahad and Lohengrin exchanged a glance before Galahad answered, ‘I know where he is.

I'll fetch him.’

As Galahad stormed through the echoing halls towards the stairs, a flash of green light

halted him in his tracks. He turned to the window, his breath catching at the sight of the

dragon. Its massive wings stretched wide, its copper scales gleaming under the night sky as

it battered the castle’s defences with a roar that shook the very stones. The shield

shimmered, defiant under the creature's wrath.

‘Foolish beast,’ Galahad muttered with a hollow laugh. ‘After five years, and still you attack,

not knowing you’ll never breach our walls.’ Shaking his head, he continued his descent.

As he passed, the humble servant William was carrying a jug of water for the queen.

Galahad barely acknowledged him, consumed in his thoughts. William moved like a ghost,

unnoticed, until he reached Guinevere’s chamber.

Pausing outside the door, William overheard Lohengrin’s voice, heavy with regret. ‘I should

have taken that poison myself.’

Guinevere’s reply was soft but firm. ‘Lohengrin, I am old. I’ve lived my life. I don’t need more

time. You have a young family. Your life is just beginning.’

With his curiosity piqued, William leaned closer, but the door suddenly swung open and he

stumbled inside.

Lohengrin and Guinevere turned towards him, their expressions softening as he awkwardly

the jug on the small table beside Guinevere.

‘William,’ Guinevere said gently, her eyes bright with mischief despite her frailty, ‘have you

asked Margaret out yet?’

William smiled shyly, shaking his head. ‘No, not yet, my lady.’

Guinevere reached out and took his hand, her touch warm. ‘When Arthur asked me out, it

was in a much darker time. But the only thing that truly matters is that you ask. A question

William’s smile widened, and he nodded. ‘You’re right. Thank you.’

Turning to Lohengrin, Guinevere’s eyes sparkled with curiosity. ‘And how did you ask Belaye

Lohengrin chuckled softly. ‘Badly. Everything went wrong, but . . . she loved that I tried.’

William, emboldened by their words, picked up the second cup on the table and said, ‘Thank

all these ingredients from the quests are for.’

Guinevere, weak but composed, sat up slightly, her eyes distant with knowing. ‘I know what

we’re making,’ she said, her voice steady despite the strain, ‘and I believe I know who it’s

for.’

Lohengrin stopped pacing and turned to her, his brow furrowed. ‘How?’ he asked.

Guinevere took a breath before beginning, ‘You may have been taught by Merlin and

Nimue—’ but she was interrupted by a fit of coughing. When she recovered, she continued,

‘But I knew them longer. Merlin spoke of a potion—one that could wipe the memory of an

entire kingdom.’

Lohengrin stepped closer, disbelief clouding his face. ‘Are you accusing Loholt? Your own

son?’

Guinevere’s expression was filled with regret. ‘Think about it. During the Malazan invasion,

all those he killed—wizards who could take on anyone’s face—they still resent him for the

lives he took. But if they couldn’t remember . . .’

Lohengrin sat beside her, the bed creaking under his weight. ‘So that’s why he left,’ he

murmured, realisation dawning in him. ‘He discovered this memory poison but knew he

needed a wizard to gather the ingredients.’

Their conversation was abruptly halted as the door creaked open. Both Lohengrin and

Guinevere turned towards it, falling silent as if caught in a spell.

Several floors below, Galahad paused outside Merlin’s chamber, his fingers tracing the cold

stone wall until he pressed a hidden stone. The wall slid aside, revealing a secret room filled

with old scrolls and forgotten texts. There Loholt sat deep in thought, his face hard with

concentration as he pored over a scroll.

Tall and imposing, his deep brown eyes gleamed with a mix of anger and contemplation.

Loholt flinched as Galahad’s voice cut through the silence. ‘What are you doing down here?

Your mother is dying upstairs, and you’re hiding here—what, reading about a future that may

never come to pass?’ The accusation hung in the air, sharp as a blade.

Loholt’s eyes flared with anger as he glared up at Galahad. ‘I am the king. I do as I please.

Right now, I believe I’ve found a prophecy—about me. I will stay here until I decipher it,’ he

snapped, his defiance palpable.

Without hesitation, Galahad grabbed Loholt by the shoulder, dragging him towards the stairs.

The prophecy scroll fell from Loholt’s grip, colliding with an invisible barrier before tumbling

to the floor, forgotten.

As they neared Guinevere’s chamber, Galahad’s grip softened. His voice, now gentler, urged

him, ‘I cannot force you to go in, but as someone who had the change to see my father one

last time before he passed, I beg you—don’t waste this moment. I was just a boy, but that

day still haunts me.’ With that, he opened the door and stepped inside.

Loholt stood frozen at the threshold, his hand trembling and his legs weakening. He took a

steadying breath before summoning the strength to enter. Inside, Lohengrin stood by the

bedside, his bloodshot eyes burning with grief. He turned sharply as Loholt entered,

brushing past him and storming out.

Galahad followed him, closing the door quietly. ‘Why did you leave?’ he asked, his voice on

the verge of breaking.

‘She was always there for us,’ Lohengrin began, his voice trembling. ‘Whenever we needed

her, she helped without hesitation. Now when I can save her, when I can help her, she won’t

even consider it.’ His voice cracked, the weight of his frustration and despair hanging in the

air.

‘She misses Arthur,’ Galahad said softly. ‘She wants to be with him again. That’s why she

won’t let you save her. You, of all people, understand that feeling.’

Lohengrin’s magic began to swirl around him, a soft glow radiating from his body as his

emotions surged. Tears welled in his eyes. ‘I have the power to heal, to pull someone back

from the brink of death,’ he whispered, his voice raw with grief, ‘but my mother is dying, and

she won’t let me save her. What’s the point of this power if I can’t use it for the people I

love?’

Unable to hold back any longer, Galahad embraced him, and the two sank to the floor, their

grief shared, as Lohengrin’s sobs filled the air. They remained there, enveloped in the

warmth of their magic and the cold weight of their sorrow as the echoes of their loss filled the

chamber.

As William lingered outside Guinevere’s bedroom, he heard the quiet shuffle of footsteps and

the soft click of the door as Lohengrin and Galahad left. Inside, Loholt moved slowly towards

his mother, his steps heavy on the creaking floor. Guinevere’s voice, though weak, cut

through the silence. ‘Loholt, why did you return home?’

Loholt sat down beside her on the bed, the old wood groaning beneath his weight. Gently, he

touched her legs and said, ‘Because you’re getting old, and I wanted to see you one more

time.’

Guinevere shifted uncomfortably, lifting her frail arms and silently asking for a hug. Loholt

smiled and leaned in to embrace her. As they held each other, she patted him softly on the

back, slipping something unseen into place.

Loholt pulled away and stood. ‘I’ll go bring Lohengrin and Galahad back,’ he said, turning

towards the door. But before he could leave, Guinevere’s voice stopped him cold.

‘What have you done to Lohengrin’s family?’

Loholt froze mid-step. Slowly, he turned around, his expression darkening. ‘What did you just

say?’

Guinevere’s gaze did not waver as she repeated, ‘I asked, what did you do to Lohengrin’s

family?’

His hand clenched into a fist, anger rippling through him. ‘I didn’t do anything!’ he snarled.

Guinevere’s eyes softened with sorrow. ‘I watched you grow up, Loholt. You don’t think I’d

recognise your handwriting? How you shape your Gs? After Lohengrin came to me after the

second quest, I had a feeling it was you. I’m sorry . . . sorry that I brought a monster like you

into this world.’

Loholt’s face twisted with fury. Without warning, he grabbed a pillow from the bed and

pressed it hard against Guinevere’s face. She struggled beneath his grip, her hands flailing

weakly as she fought to breathe. On the other side of the wall, William covered his mouth in

horror, his heart pounding as Guinevere’s struggles grew weaker and weaker until they

stopped altogether.

Loholt stood up slowly, dropping the pillow. His breath was steady as if nothing had

happened. He turned and walked towards the door with deliberate, heavy steps.

Outside, William sank to the floor, his body trembling. In a barely audible whisper, he

muttered, ‘Did Loholt just kill his mother?’

Multiverse of Time, Earth, Lublin, Poland, Year 1939, 27 September, 12am

A quaint two-storey house stood on the outskirts of Lublin, surrounded by a vast expanse of

green fields that masked the creeping chaos. The world outside was crumbling, yet the

serene surroundings seemed to defy the encroaching darkness. Inside, Edward, a young

man poised between youth and adulthood, stood by the window. His sandy hair fell messily

over his forehead as his worried eyes scanned the horizon, following distant figures

appearing and disappearing against the bleak backdrop. His heart sank as he watched four

shadowy figures sprint past the house, one momentarily locking eyes with him before

vanishing into the void of uncertainty.

In the stillness that followed, Edward turned from the window and called for his mother.

Angela, a figure of strength and solace, descended the staircase. Her presence had an

innate power to calm, and her every step was deliberate and steady even as the world

around them seemed to teeter on collapse. A bracelet on her wrist caught Edward’s eye.

The year 1935 was engraved on the metal and served as a haunting reminder of a happier

time before war stole her husband and turned their lives into a tenuous fight for survival.

Edward’s gaze lingered outside, but Angela moved to the phonograph, her actions deliberate

as she selected her favourite vinyl. The soft, crackling melody of a familiar tune filled the

room, gently dispelling the oppressive silence. With a soft smile, she took Edward by the

hand and led him into the lounge. The two swayed to the music, their steps synchronised by

an unspoken understanding that this brief dance was more than an escape—it was a defiant

act of resilience. Angela’s eyes sparkled, reflecting the bittersweet joy of cherished

memories. She began to hum, threading her voice into the melody and creating a moment

untouched by the war’s shadow.

Their peace was shattered by a sudden, forceful knock at the door. Angela froze, her smile

vanishing as fear clouded her face. The persistent knocking grew louder, more insistent, a

harsh reminder of the dangers that loomed just beyond their threshold. Urgently, she

ushered Edward into a hidden nook beneath the staircase, pressing a finger to her lips in a

silent plea for silence.

When Angela finally opened the door, two Nazi soldiers barged in without hesitation. ‘Who

lives here?’ one barked, his tone sharp and unyielding. The soldiers began tearing through

the house, rifling through drawers and overturning furniture in a ruthless search. Edward,

concealed in his hiding spot, could only watch through a narrow crack, his heart pounding

with a mixture of fear and fury.

One soldier, his gaze keen and predatory, approached Edward’s hiding place. Edward

clenched his fists, powerless to act without exposing himself. Before the soldier could

uncover him, Angela intervened, her voice steady despite the tremor in her chest. ‘My son

and husband were killed in the war during a bombing,’ she said, her lie bold and unwavering.

The soldiers exchanged a sceptical glance. The bearded one grunted and ascended the

stairs to continue searching. The other lingered, his eyes narrowing as he studied Angela.

‘You’re quite beautiful,’ he murmured, stepping closer, his intentions unmistakable.

Angela took a step back, her discomfort evident, but the soldier grabbed her shoulder,

pulling her towards him. ‘This house is too big for just one person,’ he whispered, his words

laced with malice.

With fire in her eyes, Angela replied, ‘There would be more if you weren’t here.’

The soldier’s expression darkened, his anger flaring, but before he could act, his companion

descended the stairs. ‘Let’s go,’ he muttered. As the bearded soldier left, the remaining one

tightened his grip on Angela, unleashing violence upon her. Angela’s screams pierced the

air, each one cutting through Edward like a blade.

Anger erupted within Edward, a raw and untamed force that had long simmered beneath the

surface. His hands began to glow with a mixture of red and green magic, the energy that

pulsed with his anguish and rage. Unable to contain the power, Edward unleashed it,

shattering the hiding nook as massive green wings sprouted from his back. He surged

forward, his magic tearing through the floorboards and sending splinters into the soldier’s

chest.

The second soldier burst into the room, gun drawn, but Edward’s magic bent the wood

beneath his feet, transforming it into a protective shield of metal that deflected the bullet.

With a cry of determination, Edward directed another surge of magic, sharp wooden shards

flying through the air and felling the soldier.

As the chaos subsided, Edward turned to Angela. She lay on the ground, her dress stained

with blood. A faint smile graced her face, even as life ebbed away. ‘Edward,’ she whispered,

her voice a fragile thread, ‘this world is full of angry people. Promise me you won’t become

one of them.’

Tears streamed down Edward’s face as he cradled her in his arms. ‘I promise,’ he choked

out though his heart shattered with the weight of her dying wish. Angela’s hand fell from his

cheek as her final breath escaped her lips.

Overwhelmed by loss, Edward’s scream reverberated through the crumbling house, a raw

manifestation of his sorrow. His magic surged again, reshaping the house’s remnants into an

unrecognizable form. The trauma overwhelmed him, and as the world faded into darkness,

Edward collapsed, unconscious, his final thoughts a whirlwind of pain and loss.

Edward awoke to a changed world; his body felt heavy with grief and his magic subdued but

present. The memory of Angela’s words lingered in his mind, a guiding light in the shadows

of his despair. Though the house was gone and his life forever altered, Edward resolved to

honour his mother’s final request—to channel his power towards healing rather than

destruction even in a world intent on tearing itself apart. Edward fell unconscious.
