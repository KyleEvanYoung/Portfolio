Trust Is Never Forgotten

By Kyle E Young

Just A Normal Day, Until It Wasn’t

Cole adjusts the weight of the pistol on his hip, the taser on the other side, as he walks through the silent school toward the admin office. His footsteps echo too loudly in the empty corridor. Outside, the last of the sun slants through dusty windows, a dull orange that feels wrong—too thick, too heavy—as it presses heat against his face.

He pushes open the office door.

Lucy is waiting at the front desk, arms folded, red pen tapped against her palm. She looks up and smiles, the fluorescent lights buzzing faintly above her.

“How’s it going, Cole?” she asks.

He forces a smile back. “Not much. Just did my lap of the school. Looks like no one’s here.”

Lucy raises a brow, teasing. “Oh, don’t say it like that. You mean it’s just you in this whole school… alone?”

He shrugs, trying to shake off the strange weight in the air. “Come on, work wife, just pass me the keys so I can lock up.”

She steps closer, letting the keys dangle between two fingers. “You got somewhere to be with the other woman?”

Cole huffs a quiet laugh. “No. I just want to get out of this place. I’ve already failed here once.”

Lucy’s smile softens for a moment. She moves around the desk, the ring of keys cold and heavy as she presses them into his palm. “I don’t think you would’ve failed if you’d had a teacher like me.”

He gives her a crooked grin. “No, I would’ve still failed.”

She walks past him toward the door. The air shifts when she opens it, letting in a brief smell of cut grass and hot asphalt before the hall swallows it again.

“I think you underestimate how good a teacher I am,” she says over her shoulder.

Cole turns, smirking. “I think you underestimate how pretty you are.”

Lucy blushes and shakes her head. “Well. I hope you have a good date night with Sophia.”

“And you with Michael,” he answers.

Lucy leaves, the door closing with a dull, final click. For a moment, the school feels too quiet—like the building is holding its breath.

Cole flips the keys in his hand and starts locking up, door by door. The halls seem longer now, the lights dimmer, as if the building is slowly swallowing its own light.

He reaches Lucy’s classroom—Room 13.

The door is slightly ajar.

There’s a faint murmur coming from inside, like a broken radio just on the edge of tuning in. He pushes the door open.

Inside, five girls sit in a circle on the floor, hands linked, heads bowed. Someone has painted symbols on the ground around them in rough, dark strokes—twisting shapes that make his eyes want to slide off them.

“Girls, it’s time to go,” Cole says, stepping into the room.

They don’t move.

The murmuring gets clearer as he walks closer. It isn’t English. It isn’t anything he recognizes. The words warp in his ears, scraping against something inside his head, like they’re not meant for a human throat—or a human world.

“Hey,” he says, placing a hand on the nearest girl’s shoulder. Her skin is cold. “It’s Friday. Go home, enjoy the weekend.”

No response. No flinch. Their bodies sway slightly, but it’s not natural movement—it’s like something else is moving them from inside.

“Come on,” he mutters.

He starts to shake the girl gently, then harder. Nothing. Her lips keep moving, shaping those wrong sounds. The others chant with her, voices braiding into one low, humming thread that makes the air vibrate.

Cole moves to the next girl, gripping her shoulders. “Snap out of it,” he says. “This isn’t funny.”

They ignore him, if ignoring is even the right word. It’s like he doesn’t exist.

The words grow louder. The room feels hotter. The symbols on the floor seem to pulse.

Suddenly, all five girls speak in perfect unison, the chant slamming into the air like a wave. The sound makes his teeth ache.

“Enough!” Cole barks, stepping back. He pulls out his taser, holding it up. “All of you stop and get up, or I’ll tase you. I’m not kidding.”

The chanting doesn’t falter.

Then, in the same instant, all five girls fall silent.

Their eyes snap open.

Their irises glow a deep, burning red.

“Shit,” Cole whispers.

He fires the taser at the closest girl. The prongs hit, the device crackles, but she doesn’t react. None of them do. Instead, thick, dark blood begins to seep from their eyes, tracing slow lines down their cheeks like tears. It seeps from their noses next, heavy drops splashing onto the symbols on the floor.

Cole fumbles for his phone, hands slick with sweat. He starts dialing 9-1, then hesitates, staring at the scene.

What is he supposed to say?

Hi, I’ve got five kids chanting demon words and crying blood.

His thumb hovers over the screen.

In the corner of his vision, something moves.

The girls are rising. Not standing—floating. Their feet leave the floor, shoes dangling inches, then a foot, then more above the ground. Their bodies hang limp, heads tilted back, mouths opening wide to release a new sound: a shriek that doesn’t match their throats. It’s too deep, too layered, like multiple voices screaming through each of them at once.

The lights flicker. The symbols on the floor flare red, glowing like hot coals. The paint—if it is paint—seems to crawl, rearranging itself into sharper, more precise shapes. The air stinks of iron and burnt hair.

Cole rips the taser leads free and shoves the device into his back pocket, hand already going to the gun on his belt.

In the center of the glowing circle, the air thickens. It distorts like heat haze, then splits, a dark rip opening in the space above the floor. Something pushes through from the other side—two black, curved horns forcing their way into this world.

The girls scream louder, their bodies arching in midair, fingers stretching like claws.

“Stop this now!” Cole yells, aiming his gun at the closest girl.

They don’t stop.

The horns keep emerging, inch by inch, something massive and unseen straining against the tear in reality. The sound in the room is unbearable—screams, buzzing lights, the low rumble of something trying to force its way through.

Cole swallows hard, throat dry. “God,” he mutters, voice shaking, “forgive me for my sins.”

He fires.

The shot cracks through the room, deafening. The nearest girl drops instantly, the scream dying in her throat as her body hits the floor with a heavy thud. The others wail even louder, their voices twisting higher, more inhuman.

He fires again. And again.

Each shot knocks another girl from the air. Each body falls into the circle, limbs collapsing wrong, blood streaking across the glowing symbols. With every impact, the red light dims a little.

By the time the last girl hits the floor, the symbols have gone dark. The horns shudder, then sink back into the tearing air, vanishing as the rip knits itself shut. The room snaps back into stillness, the shadows flickering one last time before settling.

Cole’s gun slips from his fingers. He drops to his knees.

For a long moment, there’s only the buzzing of the lights and the harsh sound of his own breathing. He stares at his shaking hands, then at the bodies.

Five girls.

Five students.

“What have I done?” he whispers.

Footsteps pound down the hallway, fast and uneven.

Lucy bursts into the doorway, panting, her eyes wide. She takes in the scene—the broken circle, the dark splashes across the floor, the bodies, Cole on his knees.

“What the—what happened?” she demands, voice cracking.

Cole turns his head slowly toward her, eyes empty. He tries to speak, but no words come.

Lucy steps into the room, her face draining of color. “Cole,” she says again, quieter, more frightened, “what happened?”

Before he can answer, one of the girls on the floor gasps, sucking in a ragged breath. Then another. And another.

Cole and Lucy stare as the girls start to move, fingers twitching, chests rising and falling. They reach up, dazed, touching their faces. Their hands come away slick with half-dried blood.

The symbols beneath them stay dark.

The air is still.

But Cole knows, with a cold certainty settling into his gut, that whatever answered them from the other side…

is not gone.

A Shattered Lives

Four years later, Cole settles into his seat on a plane and looks out the window. Airport workers load the last bags into the cargo hold, then drive away. When Cole looks around the cabin, he realizes something strange—

Almost every seat is empty.

He unbuckles and stands, walking up the aisle toward the flight attendant.

“Hey,” Cole asks, keeping his voice low, “where is everyone?”

The attendant gives him a tired smile. “Hi, sir. Most people on this route are usually connecting from other flights. But no planes have been able to land recently because of the horrible weather, so a lot of passengers couldn’t make it. Today it’s just a handful of people… and I’m the only attendant working this flight. If you could grab your things and come with me, I’ll get you settled.”

Cole nods and follows him. “So what’s your name?”

The man turns slightly. “Jason. Like it says on my name tag.”

Cole smirks. “What name tag?”

Jason pauses. He looks down—and realizes the tag isn’t there. He quickly pats his pockets, frowning, then finally finds it and clips it onto his shirt.

“Huh. Sorry,” Jason mutters.

Cole reaches up and opens the overhead compartment, pulling down his luggage.

“Alright,” Jason says, gesturing forward. “Follow me.”

Jason leads him toward the front staircase and up to the upper level. As they climb, Jason keeps talking like he’s trying to keep the mood light.

“Since there are only nine people on this plane, I’m upgrading everyone to first class. Makes it easier for me to keep an eye on everyone.”

Cole’s eyebrows lift. “Nice. That sounds great.”

They step into the first-class section. Cole scans the cabin.

A man sits alone with his phone, wired headphones in, completely locked into whatever he’s watching. In the corner, four teenage girls—maybe sixteen to eighteen—huddle together, giggling and whispering like the world can’t touch them.

Jason stops beside an open seat. “Here you go. This is your new seat. We’ll be taking off soon. Put your bags away and have a seat.”

Cole lifts his luggage into the overhead compartment. As he closes it, he hears an argument a few rows away.

“I don’t even want to go on this holiday,” a little girl says, her voice sharp and miserable.

A woman answers immediately, stern and exhausted. “Charlotte, Aunty Lucy was kind enough to let us join her on her holiday. Now be thankful.”

Cole freezes.

That name hits him like a punch.

He turns—and sees a woman leaning over the seats, speaking to a teenage girl.

And sitting beside them, unmistakable even after four years, is Lucy.

For a second, the world narrows into a thin, tight tunnel. Cole can’t move. Lucy can’t either. They stare at each other like they’re looking through glass.

And his mind flashes back—

The classroom.

Room 13.

The circle.

The blood.

The gun on the floor in front of his knees.

The girls waking up again.

Jason steps closer, voice suddenly concerned. “Sir? Hey—sir. Can you please sit down?”

The spell breaks.

Cole swallows hard. “Sorry,” he mutters, lowering himself into the seat.

Across the aisle, Lucy sits too, stiff and pale, like she’s just seen a ghost.

Jason walks to the front of first class and raises his voice slightly. “Alright, everyone—please remain seated while we take off.”

He straps himself into his seat. The cabin goes quiet.

Then the plane begins to move.

The Lost Moments

Cole closes his eyes while Jason runs through the safety procedure. The words blur together, turning into background noise. He opens his eyes again and stares out the window at thick clouds drifting past, endless and gray.

Lucy turns toward him. Her voice is quiet, but sharp.

“You left.”

Cole looks at her, jaw tight. “Can we not? Can we just… not do this?”

Lucy lets out a short, bitter laugh. “Not do this? No. You did something  to those girls, and then you quit. You just walked away. What the hell did you do?”

Cole glances toward the woman sitting beside Lucy. She’s listening now, pretending not to be, but her posture has gone stiff.

Cole lowers his voice. “I didn’t know what happened that afternoon. And I didn’t do anything.”

Jason’s voice comes over the speaker again, brighter this time. “Alright everyone, you’re free to move around the cabin. We’ve got twelve hours until landing.”

Cole exhales like he’s been given permission to escape. “Good. I don’t need this.”

He stands and starts down the aisle, but Lucy immediately follows. Her friend hesitates, then follows too.

“I’m not letting you walk away from me again,” Lucy says.

Cole doesn’t slow down. “I don’t care what you want.”

He heads down the stairs to the lower level. The girls’ laughter from earlier echoes somewhere behind them, distant and wrong for how tense everything suddenly feels.

Lucy keeps close. “We’re on a plane, Cole. You can’t avoid me forever.”

Cole keeps walking until he reaches the bar. He stops, grabs a bottle, and pours himself a drink with shaking hands he’s trying hard to control.

Lucy steps in front of him. “Tell me what happened that night. And why you just disappeared.”

Cole takes a long swallow.

Lucy’s friend hangs back near the corner, half-hidden, watching like she’s scared of what she might hear.

Cole stares into his glass for a moment, then says it anyway—flat and quiet.

“I shot them.”

Lucy’s face tightens.

Cole’s voice doesn’t. “I shot all of them. All the girls. I fired my gun.” He swallows, throat working like the words are sharp going down. “I became what I was supposed to protect the school from. A shooter. And when they hit the floor, I didn’t know what to do. I didn’t know what to say.”

Behind Lucy, her friend clamps a hand over her mouth. Tears spill down her face, silent and panicked—like she’s thinking about her own kid, about what this means. She takes a step back, then another, and slips away down the hallway without a word.

Lucy doesn’t move. “But you didn’t ,” she says, voice trembling with anger and disbelief. “There were no bullet holes. No wounds. They were fine, Cole. The hospital said they were dehydrated and needed vitamins—Vitamin K and Vitamin C. It took a few days, and they were back to normal.”

Cole drains half the glass in one go, then sets it down hard. “You’re not hearing me.”

Lucy’s eyes flash. “I’m hearing you.”

“No,” Cole says, shaking his head. “They were… summoning something. Someone. Their eyes were red. They were floating. The blood on the ground was glowing.”

Lucy steps closer and grips his face with both hands, forcing him to look at her. Her voice drops to a furious whisper.

“Why are you lying to me?”

Cole jerks back, breath rising. “I’m not! That’s what happened!”

Lucy’s hands fall away. She looks at him like she doesn’t recognize him anymore. “None of the girls remember anything. Do you know what it sounds like? It sounds like you drugged them, stole their blood, and painted symbols all over my classroom. And then you ran.”

Cole moves around the bar, desperate now. “But I didn’t. Those things happened. Why won’t you believe me?”

He steps toward her, but Lucy takes a step back like she’s afraid he might touch her again.

Then her expression shifts—anger cracking into something softer, sadder.

“You know what I missed the most at school?” she says quietly.

Cole stops. His eyes drop to the floor for a second, like he can’t stand the way she’s looking at him. Then he forces himself to meet her gaze.

“What?”

Lucy’s smile is small, and it doesn’t reach her eyes. “All the little moments. All the times I called you sexy. All the jokes we could make just because we could.” Her voice wavers. “All the days where the first face I saw at work was yours—always smiling like nothing could touch you.”

Cole doesn’t speak. He can’t.

Lucy keeps going, each word heavier than the last. “All the good mornings. Hundreds of them. Maybe thousands.” She blinks hard, trying not to cry. “It could’ve been forever.”

Cole frowns. “What do you mean… it could’ve been forever?”

Lucy exhales shakily. “Three years ago today, Michael broke up with me.”

The confession hangs between them for a beat, raw and unexpected.

Then Lucy turns and walks away.

Cole reaches out like he might stop her. “Wait—”

But she doesn’t.

He stands there at the bar, staring at the drink in front of him. His hand closes around the glass again.

He whispers to himself, “One more won’t hurt.”

And he drinks.

To All Forgotten Moments

Jason stands at the front of first class, running through the safety procedure. When he finishes, he smiles politely.

“You’re now free to move around the cabin.”

Cole, Lucy, and the other woman unbuckle and leave the first-class area. Jason turns toward the galley, already starting to prepare the meals.

“There’s always one,” he mutters under his breath.

He walks back into first class and stops beside a teenage girl sitting alone. He holds out a menu with a practiced smile.

“Hey. Here’s a menu with all the food we’re serving on the flight. I’ll come back in a minute.”

The girl looks down at it, eyes narrowing like she’s struggling to make out the words. Jason notices the worry on her face and gently steps in.

“So,” he says, “for lunch we’ve got chicken, beef, vegetarian pasta, and, for the first time, fake chicken.”

The girl looks up. “Fake chicken?”

Jason grins. “Yeah. It’s for vegetarians who miss the idea of meat but don’t want the real thing.”

The girl laughs. “Who doesn’t like meat?”

Jason tilts his head. “So, the beef then?”

“Yes, please,” she says.

“Great. For sides, we’ve got bread, salad, or pasta. What sounds like you?”

She brightens. “I’m a big pasta fan, so pasta, please.”

“Excellent. Sounds like a great meal.”

Jason flips to the back page of his order sheet and ticks the boxes. “What’s your name?”

“Charlotte,” she says, smiling now.

“Well, it’s nice to meet you, Charlotte.”

At the back of first class, a man gets up. Jason catches a clear look at his face and freezes. He knows that face.

“Everything okay?” Charlotte asks.

Jason forces a smile. “Yeah. Just gotta check on another passenger.”

He walks after the man, following him out of first class and down the short hallway toward the bathroom. The man goes inside, and Jason waits outside, shifting his weight from foot to foot. His leg starts to shake.

He notices the woman from earlier walk past him, heading back toward first class. The bathroom door opens behind him, and the man steps out.

Jason’s face hardens. “Not you.”

The man’s expression mirrors his. “Not you,” he answers.

“You said you’d never fly again,” Jason says quietly.

“Yeah, well,” the man replies, “I didn’t want to. But my friend died on holiday, and I’m the one who has to confirm it’s really him.”

Jason’s hand curls into a fist. “Marcus, the same shit that happened two years ago better not happen again.”

Marcus exhales through his nose. “Thirteen years ago to the day,” he says slowly, “the same thing happened. A bunch of girls in a circle, blood running from their eyes. But those girls lived. At least pouring salt on the blood stopped the spell and saved them.”

Jason stares at him. “Just promise me it won’t happen again.”

Marcus shakes his head. “I would if I could. But we didn’t make it happen. They did it themselves. And they didn’t even remember doing it.”

Before Jason can answer, the woman bursts out of first class, panic twisting her voice.

“Where is Charlotte? Where is my little girl?”

Jason looks at her, then at Marcus, and rushes back into first class with both of them following.

The cabin is wrong.

The four giggling teenage girls who were there before are gone. Charlotte’s seat is empty. Jason walks quickly to the row where she’d been sitting and finds nothing—no bag, no girl, just an abandoned seat.

He turns back to the others, mind racing.

Lucy walks into first class, frowning. “Why is everyone standing up?”

Cole steps in behind her, holding a drink. “What’s going on?”

The woman points straight at Cole, eyes blazing. “This is all your fault!”

Marcus reaches out and gently holds her back as she moves toward him.

Cole glances down at his glass and realizes it’s empty. When he looks up again, Lucy is staring at him, her face pale.

“What did you do?” she asks.

Actions Have Consequences

Cole stares at the woman, completely confused. She’s pointing at him like he’s a criminal, her arm shaking with rage. Marcus has both hands on her shoulders, holding her back, and Jason stands behind them, tense and watchful. Lucy looks at Cole with obvious disappointment.

Cole raises his hands slightly. “I haven’t done anything.”

“Yes, you have!” the woman screams. “You did before—and now my girl is gone, and it’s your fault!”

Cole turns to Lucy, trying to ground himself. “I’ve been with Lucy this whole time. I don’t know what she’s talking about.”

The woman stops struggling just long enough to spit the words out. “I heard you talking to her. You said you shot five girls at a school.”

Marcus snaps his head toward Cole. “You did what?”

Cole feels Lucy’s eyes on him, heavy and accusing. He knows exactly what will happen if he tells the truth—if he describes the chanting, the glowing symbols, the blood, the floating bodies. Lucy already thinks he’s lying. Everyone else will, too.

So he forces a different story.

“It was paint,” Cole says quickly. “Red paint. No one got hurt. It was… a prank. The girls wanted to freak Lucy out.”

Lucy blinks, then lets out a small, relieved smile. “Why didn’t you just tell me that in the first place?”

Cole smiles back, but it’s thin and painful. “Because I promised them I wouldn’t.”

The woman—Mila—looks around wildly, like the explanation doesn’t matter at all. “That doesn’t help me find my kid.”

Lucy steps forward, voice gentler now. “Mila, maybe Charlotte just went to the bathroom, or she’s hanging out with the other girls. She’s sixteen—she’s probably fine.”

Jason and Marcus exchange a look, both of them instantly uneasy.

Marcus releases Mila, and Lucy continues, “Come on. I’ll come with you. We’ll search the plane together.”

Lucy and Mila head out of first class, moving fast.

Jason nods toward Marcus and tries to sound casual. “I’ve got to grab some supplies from the back. Feel free to look at the menus.”

He sits for half a second, then stands again like he can’t stay still.

Marcus turns to Cole. “You okay, mate?”

Cole closes his eyes and wipes his face with his hand. “Yeah. Just… been a long day.”

Jason walks down the aisle, then down the stairs toward the lower sections of the plane. He keeps going—past where passengers normally stop—into the cargo-level access area.

And that’s where he sees them.

The girls.

Sitting in a circle.

Hands linked.

A ring of red blood painted across the floor.

Jason’s stomach drops.

“Shit. Shit. Shit,” he whispers. “Not again. Not again…”

He spins around and rushes back up the stairs.

When he returns to first class, he heads straight for the food cart. Marcus watches him closely, reading his face like it’s written in bold print. Jason tries to act normal, but his hands are shaking.

He grabs a container and starts stuffing handfuls of salt into his pockets.

Marcus stands immediately. “Jason…”

Jason doesn’t answer. He moves quickly out of first class. Marcus follows.

Cole watches them go, then gets up and follows too.

A moment later, Lucy returns with Mila. One look at the urgency on everyone’s faces and they follow as well.

They head down the stairs into the lower area together.

And there they are—Charlotte and the other girls—still in the circle, chanting under their breath. The sound is low and rhythmic, wrong in a way that makes the air feel thicker.

Marcus stares at them, voice tight. “How is this happening again?”

Cole steps up behind them, disbelief in his voice. “How is this happening on a plane?”

Jason’s jaw clenches. Cole pushes forward, speaking fast. “Last time I stopped it, I had a gun. I don’t know how to stop this without—”

Jason cuts him off, forcing a grim smile as he pulls the salt from his pockets. “We stopped it with salt.”

Cole looks at Marcus. “You’ve seen this before?”

Marcus takes a slow breath. “My last flight. Same date. A few years ago.”

Cole pulls out his phone, checking the screen like he needs proof. “When I saw it at the school… it was this date too. July 13th.”

Jason steps up to the circle and pours salt onto the blood ring.

Nothing happens.

He pours more. The salt scatters across the red line.

Still nothing.

Jason’s face tightens. “Last time… this worked.”

The chanting grows louder.

Lucy reaches the bottom of the stairs and freezes. Her hand flies to her mouth. She sinks to her knees like her legs have given out.

“No,” she whispers. “Not again…”

Mila breaks free and runs toward Charlotte. “Charlotte!”

The moment she gets close, a burst of red energy explodes outward from the circle.

It slams into all of them like a wave.

Cole, Lucy, Marcus, Jason—everyone is thrown backward, crashing onto the floor and into the walls. The air rings with the force of it.

The salt didn’t stop the spell.

It pissed it off.

Did We Fail?
